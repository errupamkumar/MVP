Every step has a screenshot taken from the running system, what you see, what to do, and what the system does behind the screen. A recorded video of each journey follows the written steps.

How the journeys fit together

The three people act on the same trip at the same time. Each journey is written from one person's side and links to the moment another person acts.

  1. The admin opens the desk; two riders are already waiting (A2–A5).
  2. The driver signs on; the waiting riders are matched (D5).
  3. The user books and takes the last seat (U9).
  4. The driver boards everyone by OTP and drops them (D7–D11).
  5. The user rates the ride; the desk sees the cost and audit trail (U16, A13).

Driver: from start of duty to sign-off

Do
Enter DRV-2281 and Plant@123, then tap Sign in.
What happens
The app opens the driver side (Trips, Issues, Duty). The server address in use is printed at the bottom, which helps when a phone cannot connect.
D2

Start-of-duty check: documents

The driver's own documents come first: driving licence valid to 04/2030 and gate pass valid to 01/2027, both green. Below, the vehicle list with C-12 preselected and an amber note: PUC certificate expires in 6 days.

Do
Check the documents. Nothing to tap if they are green.
What happens
Compliance is checked here, before dispatch, not at the gate. An expired document blocks duty outright (see DX1).
D3

Choose the vehicle

Vehicles that cannot be used are greyed out, each with the reason: C-21 on duty with A. Singh, C-44 is off-road, shuttle S-03 on duty with R. Das. C-15 is free.

Do
Keep C-12 selected.
What happens
A vehicle with an expired insurance or permit is never offered, so an unfit vehicle cannot go on duty.
D4

Vehicle check and opening odometer

Five checks with large tick boxes (usable with gloves on): tyres, lights, seat belts, fuel, first-aid kit and extinguisher. The opening odometer is filled from the last recorded reading, 41,208 km.

Do
Tick all five checks, confirm the odometer, tap Sign on for duty.
What happens
An incomplete check is refused by the server. The opening reading starts the day's distance log, used at sign-off (D15).

What happens
M. Iyer and S. Banerjee were already waiting at Gate 2. The matching engine pooled them onto C-12 the moment the driver signed on. The sequence comes from the route master, not the driver.

Desk view at this moment: A6.

Nothing. New pickups appear by themselves in route order.

Issues and end of duty

D12

Issues and SOS

The Issues tab: Breakdown, Accident, Delay, Fuel entry or Other, a details box, and Send to transport desk. The cab goes off-road and your riders are moved to another cab.

Do
Pick what happened, describe it, tap Send to transport desk. (Not sent in this walkthrough, so the demo data stays clean.)
What happens
A breakdown takes the cab off-road, puts its riders back in the queue from the last stop reached, and raises a desk alert.
D13

Duty summary

The Duty tab shows the driver's documents and the current duty: C-12, start time, duty hours against the 9-hour limit, 1 trip, 3 riders, 3.4 km, opening odometer 41,208 km.

Do
Open Duty at the end of the shift.
What happens
Duty hours are counted continuously; the engine will not give a driver a trip that would take them past the limit.
D14

Close duty

Close duty asks for the closing odometer and, optionally, fuel drawn today.

Do
Enter closing odometer 41214 and fuel 2.5 litres, then tap Sign off duty.
What happens
The server compares the odometer distance with the GPS distance recorded on trips.
D15

Signed off

Duty closed for C-12: 1 trip completed, 3.4 km GPS distance, 3 riders carried, and a green check: GPS km matches the odometer. Odometer 6 km vs GPS 3.4 km on trips.

Do
Nothing more. The driver is off duty.
What happens
This record is what the vendor bill and the fuel log are checked against (A13). The desk audit trail records duty ended.

Review

How this was checked, and what it found

The screenshots were taken in one continuous scripted run of all three journeys on the real web build (Spring Boot API and MySQL, Expo web app), in the order things happen. Each screenshot was then reviewed against the step text. The videos were recorded in separate runs, one per journey, each starting from a demo reset.
